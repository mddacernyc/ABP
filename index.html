Food insecurity is a problem that requires collective action - and for people managing food allergies, intolerances, or Celiac Disease, a "stocked" pantry can still mean nothing safe to eat. AnnaBelle's Pantry runs community food drives for gluten-, dairy-, and nut-free groceries, and gets them into local pantries through Island Harvest.

Who's Behind the Pantry

AnnaBelle & Michael

A father-daughter project that started as one Gold Award and grew into a regional food network.

Founder

AnnaBelle

Diagnosed with Celiac Disease at age six, after her pediatrician noted she "was not growing or gaining weight." Celiac is an autoimmune condition that makes gluten harmful to the small intestine - and it makes safe nutrition anything but automatic.

AnnaBelle now leads a Pantry chapter from New England while she's in college. The whole project began as her Girl Scout Gold Award: a cookbook of naturally gluten-free recipes, built around the affordable ingredients you'd actually find in a food pantry.

President - Formerly Outreach & Volunteer Coordinator

Michael

Started as the Pantry's Outreach and Volunteer Coordinator and now serves as President, running the drives, partner relationships, and logistics that keep donations moving.

Under his outreach, AnnaBelle's Pantry now delivers to more than 50 food banks across New England, the Mid-Atlantic, and the South.

It started in the winter of 2020, in the middle of the COVID-19 pandemic. Layoffs and job loss were pushing more families toward food pantries - just as AnnaBelle and Michael noticed something the shelves weren't accounting for: allergy-friendly food costs far more than the food it replaces, and almost none of it was showing up in donation bins. So they started running their own drives, one grocery store table and one library lobby at a time.

"It is only through advocacy and hard work that we can truly make an impact on the world around us."

Gold Award Project

Gluten Free On a Dime

AnnaBelle's original Gold Award cookbook collects naturally gluten-free recipes built around ingredients you can actually find in a food pantry - no specialty flours required. It lives on its own site, Gluten Free On a Dime.

Run Your Own

Host a Drive

At your school, public library, or local grocery store.

01

Email us to schedule

Write to celiacadvocacy@gmail.com with your location and a couple of date options.

02

Get your materials

We'll send an information sheet of donation suggestions, a sign for your donation box, and an editable flyer to advertise your event.

03

We spread the word

Your drive gets listed on our Upcoming Events page and shared on Instagram.

04

Collect donations

Gather contributions for the length of your scheduled drive.

05

Deliver or arrange pickup

Box or bag what you've collected. Island Harvest is right off Exit 49 of the LIE in Melville, NY - drop off yourself, or we'll arrange a pickup.

Not on Long Island? Donate directly to your own local food pantry instead - the goal is safe food on shelves near you, wherever that is.
